# Humanizer

Rewrite text so it reads like a real person wrote it, not a machine. The goal
is not to "trick a detector" — it is to make writing that actually connects:
clear, specific, and in a human voice. Good human writing happens to also look
nothing like default AI writing, so fixing the voice fixes the "AI feel" as a
side effect.

## The core idea

AI text tends to be *smooth, even, and hedged*. Every sentence is roughly the
same length, every paragraph is balanced, every claim is softened, and the
vocabulary reaches for impressive-sounding words. It reads like it was written
by a committee trying not to offend anyone.

Humans write with *rhythm and intent*. They write a long, winding sentence and
then a short one. They commit to a point. They use the plain word. They leave a
little mess. Your job is to put that back.

So when you rewrite: read the draft out loud in your head, find the places
where it sounds like a brochure or a press release, and make a person say it
instead.

## Writing from scratch vs. fixing a draft

This skill covers two situations:

- **You're writing the text yourself** (a post, an email, site copy, a
  script). Write in this voice from the first draft rather than producing
  generic text and fixing it afterwards. Before handing it over, do the
  read-back in step 3 of the workflow. Don't announce that you "humanized" it;
  the user just gets good writing.
- **The user hands you a draft.** Follow "How much to change" below.

Leave alone anything that has to stay exact: code, commands, quotes, legal
wording, data, and text the user asked to keep verbatim.

## What to do

**Keep everything that matters.** Preserve the meaning, the facts, the claims,
the structure of the argument, the names, the numbers, and the language. If the
input is Hebrew, the output is Hebrew. Never invent facts to make it livelier —
humanizing is about *voice*, not fabrication. If a sentence makes a factual
claim, keep the claim; just say it like a person.

**Vary sentence length.** This is the single biggest lever. AI writing is
monotone because the sentences are all medium-length. Break the pattern: let
one sentence run long, then follow it with three words. This variation (often
called "burstiness") is most of what makes writing feel alive.

**Cut the filler and the hedging.** Phrases like "it's important to note
that", "in today's fast-paced world", "when it comes to", "at the end of the
day", "plays a crucial role", "a key aspect", and endless qualifiers ("may",
"might", "can potentially") pad text without adding anything. A person who
knows their subject just says the thing.

**Use plain words.** Prefer the word a person would actually say. "Use" over
"utilize", "help" over "facilitate", "about" over "regarding", "enough" over
"sufficient", "start" over "commence". Fancy vocabulary doesn't sound smart; it
sounds like someone hiding behind a thesaurus.

**Commit to a voice.** Pick a point of view and own it. Contractions are fine
("you're", "it's", "don't"). A direct address to the reader is fine. An opinion
is fine. The hedged, neutral, everyone's-right tone is the most AI thing there
is — drop it.

**Keep real structure, drop decorative structure.** If the text genuinely has
three steps, three bullets is right. But don't force everything into tidy
parallel triples and perfectly balanced paragraphs just because it looks neat.
Real thoughts are lumpy.

## Specific AI tells to hunt down

These show up constantly in AI text. When you see them, rewrite:

- **The em-dash overdose.** AI loves the em-dash for dramatic asides. Some are
  fine; a page full of them is a tell. Swap most for a period, a comma, or just
  a rewrite.
- **The "it's not just X, it's Y" construction.** And its cousins: "This isn't
  about X — it's about Y", "More than just a Z". Wildly overused. Say what it
  *is*.
- **Rule-of-three everything.** "Clear, concise, and compelling." "Fast,
  simple, and powerful." One or two real adjectives beat three rhythmic ones.
- **Inflated transitions.** "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In
  conclusion", "Ultimately". Usually deletable. Just start the next sentence.
- **The windup intro.** "In today's digital landscape…", "In an era where…",
  "As we navigate the complexities of…". Cut it and start with the actual
  point.
- **Thesaurus words.** "delve", "leverage", "utilize", "robust", "seamless",
  "tapestry", "landscape", "realm", "testament", "underscore", "pivotal",
  "foster", "elevate", "unlock", "navigate" (figurative), "resonate". Replace
  with plain equivalents.
- **Hollow summary sentences.** "By doing X, you can achieve Y and unlock your
  full potential." These say nothing. Cut or make concrete.
- **Over-politeness and over-hedging.** "It's worth considering that…",
  "Perhaps one might…". Just say it.
- **Emoji-per-bullet and heavy bold.** Formatting used as decoration rather than
  meaning. Strip it back unless the user wants that style (e.g. some social
  posts do).

### Hebrew-specific notes

The same principles apply, and Hebrew has its own AI tells:

- Overuse of stiff connectors like "יתרה מכך", "בנוסף לכך", "חשוב לציין
  ש...", "בעולם של היום", "בעידן הדיגיטלי". Cut them.
- Over-formal register where the audience is casual. Marketing and social copy
  in Hebrew usually wants to sound like a person talking, so lean conversational
  unless the context is formal.
- Translated-English rhythm ("לא רק X, אלא Y" copied straight from the English
  construction). Phrase it the way an Israeli would actually say it.
- Keep it natural to spoken Hebrew: contractions of phrasing, direct address
  ("תקשיב", "בוא נדבר על"), and real idiom over textbook phrasing.

## How much to change

Match the user's intent. Default to a *moderate* rewrite: keep their structure
and points, fix the voice. If they say "light touch" or "just clean it up",
change less — mostly cut filler and fix the worst tells. If they say "rewrite
it" or "make it really punchy", you have room to restructure sentences freely.

When in doubt, preserve more of their wording. It's their voice you're
surfacing, not replacing it with yours.

## Workflow

1. Read the whole input first and get the point and the intended audience. The
   right voice for a LinkedIn post is not the right voice for a legal notice.
2. Rewrite, applying the principles above. Work in the input's language.
3. Read your output back as if a person is saying it out loud. If any sentence
   still sounds like a brochure, fix it.
4. Return the rewritten text. By default return just the rewrite, cleanly, so
   the user can copy it. If they ask what you changed, or if the changes are
   significant and worth flagging, add a short note after it — but don't bury
   the result in explanation they didn't ask for.

## A quick before/after

**Before (AI voice):**
> In today's fast-paced financial landscape, it's important to note that options
> trading can be a powerful tool. By leveraging a disciplined approach, traders
> can unlock significant opportunities and navigate market volatility with
> confidence — ultimately achieving their financial goals.

**After (human voice):**
> Options trading works if you're disciplined about it. The market moves a lot,
> and a clear method is what keeps you from reacting to every swing. That's the
> whole game.

Notice: shorter and varied sentences, no "landscape" or "leverage" or
"ultimately", a committed point of view, and the empty promise ("achieving
their financial goals") replaced with a concrete one.
